Architectural Monograph

The Anatomy of a Levantine Villa

Centuries before mechanical HVAC systems, Mediterranean and Middle Eastern architects perfected passive thermodynamic design through spatial choreography.

Element 01•Spatial Heart

The Courtyard (Hosh)

Serving as a thermal chimney, the central court traps cool night air and disperses daytime radiant heat. Framed by citrus trees and water features, it guarantees perpetual seclusion from exterior visual sightlines.

Thermal Delta−6°C Natural Cooling
Element 02•Air Flow Corridors

The Shaded Loggia (Liwan)

A monumental three-sided vaulted salon open entirely to the prevailing summer breezes. It blurs domestic interiority with garden tranquility, establishing an effortless transition between entertaining salons and shaded gardens.

Air CirculationCross-Ventilation Direct
Element 03•Earth Materiality

Thermal Stone Mass (Hajar)

Heavy 50cm quarried limestone and basalt perimeter walls absorb diurnal heat cycles slowly, keeping interiors naturally temperate through scorching mid-day desert sun and releasing warmth during cool Levant autumn nights.

Acoustic & ThermalHigh Latency Inertia
